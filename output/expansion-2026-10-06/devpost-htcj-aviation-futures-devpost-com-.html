About the challenge

Main Description

DON’T PITCH THE FUTURE. PROVE IT.

PROOF Aviation Futures™, powered by Hack the Concrete Jungle (HTCJ), is an innovation challenge for founders, engineers, students, researchers, entrepreneurs, and multidisciplinary teams building the next generation of aviation, aerospace, and mobility technology.

We’re looking for more than ideas. We want to see what you can build, demonstrate, validate, and move toward real-world application.

The challenge invites teams to tackle opportunities across artificial intelligence, intelligent flight systems, aerospace, advanced air mobility (AAM/eVTOL), drones and UAS, autonomous systems, airport technology, cybersecurity and dual-use technology, cargo and logistics, passenger experience and accessibility, aviation workforce development, and aviation entrepreneurship.

From Problem to PROOF

The challenge follows a simple model:

PROBLEM → BUILD → SUBMIT → REVIEW → PROVE → MATCH → PILOT-READY

THE ROAD TO OCTOBER 25 — PROOF BOSTON ONE-DAY FLAGSHIP

This challenge feeds the PROOF Boston™ one-day flagship: Sunday, October 25, 2026, in Boston, MA.

October 25 is not a build day. Build now — choose a problem, build your solution, document your evidence. Evidence lock: October 23. Top teams prove it LIVE in Boston: tested and challenged by problem owners, demonstrated on stage, connected to investors and corporate partners in deal rooms, and converted into pilots and next actions.

Challenge Intake → Team/Problem Matching → Pre-Event Build → Evidence → Oct 25 PROOF Day → Validation → Deal Room → Pilot/Capital/Partnership → Follow-through

RSVP for the Boston flagship: luma.com/ctwex9eq

Challenge Areas

Projects may address:

AI + Flight Intelligence — predictive systems, AI copilots, decision support, operational intelligence, telemetry, maintenance, and safety.

Autonomy + Advanced Air Mobility — eVTOL, drones, autonomous flight, UAS operations, routing, fleet systems, and emerging mobility.

Airport + Aviation Infrastructure — smart airports, ground operations, charging, energy, logistics, baggage, cargo, facilities, and infrastructure.

Cybersecurity + Dual-Use Technology — aircraft and airport cybersecurity, resilient communications, data protection, trusted AI, and technologies with commercial and public-sector applications.

Passenger Experience + Accessibility — travel technology, accessibility, wayfinding, customer experience, connected journeys, and inclusive aviation.

Workforce + Aviation Business — training, maintenance, operations, education, entrepreneurship, aviation services, local suppliers, and future-of-work solutions.

Beyond the Challenge

For HTCJ, submission is not necessarily the end of the process.

Selected projects may be considered for continued opportunities within the HTCJ and PROOF ecosystem, including founder assessment, technology showcases, mentor engagement, business and industry introductions, corporate challenge matching, commercialization support, and pilot-readiness evaluation.

The objective is to identify technologies and founders capable of moving from an innovation challenge toward something that can be demonstrated, evaluated, and potentially deployed in the real world.

HACK → INCUBATE → DEPLOY

Who Should Participate?

We welcome:

Founders • Developers • Engineers • Designers • Researchers • Students • Aviation Professionals • Entrepreneurs • Product Builders • AI Teams • Hardware Teams • Robotics Teams • Aerospace Teams • Multidisciplinary Teams

You do not need to be an aviation industry veteran. If your technology can solve a meaningful aviation problem, we want to see what you can build.

What Are We Looking For?

Strong submissions should demonstrate:

REAL PROBLEM. A clearly defined aviation or aerospace need.

REAL SOLUTION. Technology or a business solution capable of addressing it.

REAL PROOF. A prototype, product, demonstration, technical architecture, working model, or other credible evidence.

REAL USER. A clear understanding of who needs the solution.

REAL PATHWAY. A reasonable next step toward testing, validation, commercialization, or deployment.

Get started

Get Started

READY TO BUILD THE FUTURE OF FLIGHT?

PROOF Aviation Futures™ is built for people ready to move beyond ideas and show what their technology can actually do.

Whether you’re a founder, engineer, developer, researcher, student, designer, aviation professional, or multidisciplinary team, this is your opportunity to identify a real aviation problem, build a solution, and prove it.

HOW TO GET STARTED

1. CHOOSE A PROBLEM
Identify a meaningful challenge across aviation, aerospace, AI, autonomy, airports, cybersecurity, passenger experience, infrastructure, logistics, workforce, or advanced air mobility.

2. BUILD YOUR SOLUTION
Create a prototype, application, AI system, hardware concept, platform, technical architecture, business solution, or working demonstration.

3. CREATE YOUR TEAM
Participate individually or bring together the technical, business, design, aviation, and industry expertise needed to strengthen your solution.

4. SUBMIT YOUR PROOF
Show us the problem, your solution, how it works, who needs it, what you built, and what you need to move forward.

5. GET REVIEWED
Submissions will be evaluated for problem relevance, innovation, technical execution, usability, business potential, demonstration readiness, and pathway toward real-world application.

6. KEEP BUILDING
Selected projects may be considered for continued opportunities through the HTCJ and PROOF ecosystem, including showcases, founder development, mentor engagement, technology matching, industry introductions, commercialization support, and pilot-readiness evaluation.

What You’ll Need

Be prepared to submit your project name, team information, problem statement, solution, technology used, target customer/user, project stage, written description, images or screenshots, demonstration/video where available, and links to your working product, prototype, repository, or supporting materials.

Start With What You Have

 

You do not need a fully developed company or finished commercial product to participate.

You do need a clear problem, a credible solution, and enough evidence for reviewers to understand what you built and why it matters.

What to Submit

What to Submit

SHOW US THE PROOF.

Your submission should make it easy for reviewers to understand the problem, what you built, how it works, who needs it, and what happens next.

Your PROOF Aviation Futures™ submission should include:

1. PROJECT NAME
Give your solution a clear, memorable name.

2. THE PROBLEM
What aviation, aerospace, airport, mobility, workforce, logistics, safety, or passenger problem are you solving? Who experiences it, and why does it matter?

3. YOUR SOLUTION
Explain what you built and how it addresses the problem.

4. YOUR PROOF
Show evidence that the solution exists. This may include a working prototype, application, AI model, hardware prototype, simulation, technical architecture, dashboard, platform, service workflow, business model, or other demonstrable solution.

5. DEMO
Whenever possible, include a short video demonstrating your project. Show the product working rather than only describing it.

6. IMAGES / SCREENSHOTS
Include clear visuals of your product, interface, hardware, prototype, diagrams, system architecture, or user experience.

7. TECHNOLOGY USED
Tell us what you built with: AI models, APIs, programming languages, cloud infrastructure, hardware, sensors, robotics, datasets, aviation systems, development platforms, or other technologies.

8. TARGET USER / CUSTOMER
Who would actually use or purchase this solution?

Examples include airlines, airports, aircraft operators, pilots, maintenance organizations, cargo companies, drone operators, travelers, government agencies, aviation businesses, schools, manufacturers, or aviation workers.

9. CURRENT STAGE
Tell us where the project stands:

Concept → Prototype → MVP → Demo Ready → Pilot Ready → Commercial

10. BUSINESS / DEPLOYMENT CASE
Explain how your solution could eventually be tested, deployed, purchased, licensed, integrated, or operated.

11. WHAT YOU NEED NEXT
Tell HTCJ what could move the project forward:

Customers • Pilot Partner • Technical Support • Aviation Expertise • Mentors • Data • Infrastructure • Corporate Partner • Capital Readiness • Workforce • Commercialization Support

12. TEAM
Introduce the people behind the project and the skills each team member brings.

Required Submission Materials

At minimum, submit:

Project description + problem statement + solution + project stage + team information + supporting visual(s).

Whenever available, also include:

Demo video • Live product link • Prototype • GitHub/repository • Pitch deck • Technical documentation • Architecture diagram • Research • Business model

Your Submission Should Answer One Question

WHAT CAN YOU PROVE?

We are not evaluating who can write the biggest pitch.

We want to understand:

WHAT DID YOU BUILD?
DOES IT SOLVE A REAL PROBLEM?
CAN WE SEE IT WORK?
WHO NEEDS IT?

Judging Criteria

  • 1. Problem Relevance — 20%
    How clearly does the project define and address a meaningful aviation, aerospace, mobility, airport, logistics, safety, workforce, or passenger problem?
  • 2. Innovation + Differentiation — 15%
    How original is the solution? Judges consider creative technology use, differentiation from existing approaches, and whether it provides a meaningful improvement.
  • 3. Technical Execution — 20%
    How well is the solution built? Judges consider functionality, engineering quality, AI/ML or hardware implementation, integrations, usability, and technical feasibility.
  • 4. PROOF / Demonstration — 20%
    Does the team provide credible proof that the solution works through a prototype, MVP, demo, simulation, test results, model output, research, or other working evidence?
  • 5. Pilot Readiness + Feasibility — 15%
    Could the solution realistically be tested or deployed? Judges consider operational practicality, safety, regulatory awareness, integration needs, scalability, and next steps.
  • 6. Impact + Commercial Potential — 10%
    What could the solution change if implemented? Consider safety, efficiency, accessibility, sustainability, workforce impact, customer value, scalability, and commercial potential.
